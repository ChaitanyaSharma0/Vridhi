4 raw collections → NLP engine

The core of the system. One extraction function runs over job descriptions and resumes alike and turns free text into a clean list of skills. Structured results go back to MongoDB; embeddings go to ChromaDB for semantic search.

Stage 1 · Extraction

Two passes. A dictionary match against ESCO catches the common skills fast. A Groq LLM pass picks up the emerging and niche ones the dictionary misses.

Details

Stage 2 · Normalisation

A curated set of 500+ skills from the EU's ESCO framework. Every spelling of a skill resolves to one canonical name, so "ML" and "machine learning" are counted together.

Details

Stage 3 · Vectors

MiniLM-L6-v2 turns skills and text chunks into 384-dimension vectors. Related skills land close together, which powers matching and RAG retrieval.

processed records + vectors → intelligence modules

Four modules read the processed data and each answers a different question: what is trending, how far am I from the role I want, what can I earn, and which other roles do I already fit. Each one is trained on Indian data.

What's trending?

Counts skill mentions per month by role and city, flags skills as rising or declining past ±15%, and forecasts six months ahead with ARIMA and linear regression.

Details

How far am I?

Compares a resume against the ideal skill vector for the target role, built from every matching posting. Missing skills are ranked by how many of those postings actually ask for them.

Details

What am I worth?

An ensemble of Random Forest, XGBoost and gradient boosting predicts the salary range for your whole profile: skills, experience and city together. SHAP values show which parts of the profile move it.

Salary disclaimer. Predicted salaries are market benchmarks derived from aggregated industry data for a given skill set, experience level, and location. They represent what the market is currently paying — not a job offer or guarantee. The purpose is to help users understand their market worth so they can negotiate fair compensation based on real industry standards.

Details

What else do I fit?

Scores your skills against every role in the market, not only the one you picked. If you are over-qualified for your target, or a closer fit somewhere that pays more, it says so, with live posting counts and the range you could expect there.

module outputs → counselor, dashboard, API

Where people meet the system. The counselor lets the LLM pick which module to call through function calling, so there is no hand-written routing. Streamlit is the dashboard, and FastAPI exposes every module to other platforms.

Talk to it

Groq reads the question, calls TalentMatch, CompIntel, RoleFit or SkillRadar as needed, pulls supporting context from ChromaDB, and answers only from what came back.

Details
  1. query"I know Python and SQL. How well do I match Data Scientist?"
  2. intentGroq decides to call TalentMatch and CompIntel
  3. toolsTalentMatch → 42% fit, gaps ML, TensorFlow, Stats. CompIntel → ₹14–17L today. RoleFit → Data Analyst 74%
  4. retrieveChromaDB → "ML appears in 88% of DS postings in India…"
  5. answerResponse built only from those numbers

Sample exchange

You

I know Python and SQL. How well do I match a Data Scientist role, and what should I learn next?

Vriddhi

You cover 42% of what Data Scientist postings ask for. At that level, expect around ₹14–17L in Bangalore.

Learn these next:

  1. Machine Learning asked for in 88% of postings
  2. TensorFlow asked for in 72%
  3. Statistics asked for in 68%

Meanwhile, you already fit 74% of Data Analyst postings, 2,800 open in Bangalore.

Sources: TalentMatch, CompIntel, RoleFit, ChromaDB
